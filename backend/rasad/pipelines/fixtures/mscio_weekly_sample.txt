MSCIO WEEKLY REPORT 3
ATTACK/SUSPIPCIIROAUCSY ACTIVITY
• TYPE OF INCIDENT: ATTACK.
• LOCATION: SoH.
• RELEVANT INFORMATION RELATED WITH THE EVENT:
On 9 October, MSCIO issued ALERT 135/26 following a reported attack against a tanker in the
Arabian Gulf. Smoke was observed coming from the vessel, and a subsequent report indicated a
large fire onboard, with a tug on scene.
On 11 October, MSCIO issued ALERT 136/26 following a reported attack against a tanker conducting
an outbound transit of the Strait of Hormuz on 10 October. The vessel was struck on the port side by
an unknown projectile, causing a fire onboard, which was subsequently extinguished. All crew were
reported safe.
On 11 October, MSCIO issued ALERT 137/26 following a reported attack against a tanker in the
Strait of Hormuz. The vessel was struck on the port side by an unknown projectile, sustaining
damage to the steering gear room and a port-side water tank. All crew were reported safe, and the
vessel proceeded towards Fujairah.
On 12 October, MSCIO issued ALERT 138/26 following a reported attack against a tanker near Sohar
Anchorage, Oman. The vessel was struck by a UAV, causing a fire onboard. Local authorities assisted
with crew evacuation, and two Indian seafarers were subsequently reported missing.
On 13 October, MSCIO issued ALERT 139/26 following a time-late report of an attack against a
tanker conducting an outbound transit of the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October. The vessel was
reportedly struck by an unknown projectile. All crew were reported safe.
On 13 October, MSCIO issued ALERT 140/26 following a time-late report of an attack against a
container vessel conducting an inbound transit of the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October. The vessel
was reportedly struck by an unknown projectile.

MSCIO WEEKLY REPORT 5
ASSESSMENT AND GUIDANCE FOR AS/SoH/GoO
• The maritime security environment in the Arabian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman remains highly volatile. The regional threat level in the Strait
of Hormuz is currently assessed as SEVERE, with deliberate hostile action considered highly likely under current conditions.
• Commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remained at reduced levels, with vessels transiting via both the southern Omani corridor and the
northern Iranian-controlled route. Operator caution persisted following previous attacks, and several companies continued to delay or reschedule
passages.
• The risk of drifting or uncharted mines within and adjacent to the Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) remains. Mine danger areas remain active, while GNSS
interference and AIS anomalies continue to be consistently reported across the Strait of Hormuz, Gulf of Oman and Arabian Gulf.
• Merchant vessels should:
- Maintain heightened situational awareness throughout all approaches and transits.
- Expect increased VHF hails, surveillance activity and possible UAS overflights.
- Consider the operational implications of AIS transmission in accordance with company procedures and applicable regulations.
- Report suspicious activity or relevant information immediately to MSCIO and UKMTO.
MSCIO WEEKLY REPORT 6
ASSESSMENT AND GUIDANCE FOR RED SEA/BeM
• The maritime threat level in the Gulf of Aden, Bab el Mandeb and Southern Red Sea is currently assessed as SUBSTANTIAL, following the two confirmed
Houthi attacks against a Saudi-flagged tanker. The incident demonstrated a renewed capability and willingness to target merchant shipping, and further attacks
are assessed as a strong possibility.
• Commercial traffic continues to transit steadily through the Southern Red Sea and Bab el Mandeb.
• Houthi messaging continues to assert that the Bab el Mandeb is closed to Saudi-affiliated vessels, reinforcing hostile intent towards vessels perceived as
linked to Saudi interests. Regional reporting indicates that missile and drone systems remain forward deployed near Bab el Mandeb, maintaining the capability
to disrupt commercial shipping should regional tensions escalate.
• It is advised for merchant vessels to:
- Maintain navigation through international waters for as long as possible.
- Navigate closer to the African coastline where feasible and operationally appropriate.
- Maintain close coordination with MSCIO, UKMTO and relevant maritime security reporting centres.
- Conduct voyage-specific risk assessments and strictly adhere to BMP-MS.
- Review vessel affiliations, cargo exposure and ownership considerations prior to transit.
- Maintain enhanced vigilance and report suspicious activity immediately to MSCIO and UKMTO.

REGISTRATION AND REPORTING
REGISTRATION AND REPORTING.
CSO's and Masters are encouraged to register their vessels upon entering the UKMTO Voluntary Reporting Area with both:
- MSCIO (https://mscio.eu/reporting/vessel-registration/)
- UKMTO (https://www.ukmto.org/reporting-formats/initial-report)
And to report all incidents to UKMTO and MSCIO.
When safe to do so, vessels should document incidents and suspicious activity through logs, photographs, video, and radar footage. CSOs should gather
information on Pattern of Life and Maritime Situational Awareness for their planned routes and ports of call to support risk assessments. These procedures
enable effective monitoring and resource allocation by CMF and EUNAVFOR ATALANTA.
